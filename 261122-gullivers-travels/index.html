Gulliver's Travels, explained

Four voyages, four very strange countries, and one ship's doctor whose view of the human race changes completely along the way. This guide walks through each story, shows how they fit together, and quizzes you as you go.

Before the voyages

Meet Lemuel Gulliver

The whole book is told by Gulliver himself, as if it were a true travel diary. That's part of the joke.

Gulliver grew up in Nottinghamshire, studied at Emmanuel College in Cambridge, and trained as a surgeon in London. He marries Mary Burton, but his medical practice struggles, so he keeps going back to sea as a ship's doctor.

He is honest, curious, a quick learner of languages and very proud of England. Hold on to that last part. The book is really about what happens to that pride.

The key idea: satire Jonathan Swift was an Irish clergyman and political writer. He invented these lands so he could make fun of real things: English and European politics, pointless wars, scientists with silly schemes, and human pride itself. Every country Gulliver visits is a kind of mirror held up to us.

Swift's world

Lilliput and the Houyhnhnm land lie in the southern Indian Ocean near Australia. Brobdingnag is a huge peninsula off North America. Laputa, Balnibarbi and Luggnagg float or sit in the Pacific east of Japan.

The big trick of Parts I & II

Same man, two sizes

Swift uses one simple ratio: 12 to 1. In Lilliput, Gulliver is twelve times taller than everyone. In Brobdingnag, everyone is twelve times taller than him.

Switching the scale changes how things look. From up high, the Lilliputians' wars and court games look silly and small. From down low, Gulliver can see every ugly pore on a giant's skin, and the giant king sees how petty Gulliver's boasting is.

I
Part I · A Voyage to Lilliput

The giant among tiny people

Shipwrecked, Gulliver wakes up tied to the ground by hundreds of threads. He becomes the emperor's most powerful weapon, and then his most dangerous embarrassment.

8 chaptersShip: the AntelopeStay: 9 months, 13 daysHis nickname: Quinbus Flestrin, the "Man-Mountain"

The story in eight beats

  1. Ch. 1

    Shipwreck and capture

    The Antelope hits a rock. Gulliver swims ashore, falls asleep, and wakes pinned down by tiny ropes while people six inches tall shoot arrows at him.

  2. Ch. 1–2

    Carried to the capital

    Drugged with sleeping potion in his wine, he's pulled on a giant cart by 1,500 horses to Mildendo and chained inside an old temple.

  3. Ch. 2

    Pockets searched

    Officials make a hilarious inventory of his things (his comb is "a sort of engine" with twenty long poles "resembling the pallisados" of the palace, and his watch might be "the god that he worships"). His sword and pistols are taken.

  4. Ch. 3

    Court games and freedom

    He watches officials win jobs by rope-dancing and silk threads by leaping over sticks. He is freed on conditions, such as not trampling anyone.

  5. Ch. 4–5

    The egg war and the stolen fleet

    He learns of the war with Blefuscu, wades across the channel and drags away fifty warships. He's made a Nardac, but refuses to help enslave Blefuscu.

  6. Ch. 5

    The palace fire

    The empress's rooms catch fire. Gulliver puts it out the only way he can: by urinating on it. The empress is disgusted and never forgives him.

  7. Ch. 7

    Charged with treason

    His enemies, Admiral Bolgolam and Treasurer Flimnap, plot against him. The "lenient" sentence is to put out his eyes. Warned by a friend, he escapes to Blefuscu.

  8. Ch. 8

    Home with tiny cattle

    He finds a real boat washed ashore, sails off, is picked up by an English ship, and makes money showing his miniature sheep and cows in England.

Who's who at the Lilliputian court

  • The Emperor: proud, warlike, and "taller by almost the breadth of my nail" than his courtiers.
  • Flimnap, the Treasurer: best rope-dancer, worries about Gulliver's food bill, becomes an enemy.
  • Skyresh Bolgolam, the Admiral: Gulliver's "mortal enemy" from the start.
  • Reldresal, Secretary: Gulliver's friend, who still proposes blinding him as the "merciful" option.
Explainer

The great egg war (and the shoe-heel parties)

Long ago, a Lilliputian prince cut his finger breaking an egg at the big end, so the emperor ordered everyone to break eggs at the small end. People who refused, the Big-Endians, rebelled six times and fled to Blefuscu, which now backs them in a war that has lasted "six-and-thirty moons." Eleven thousand people have died rather than break their eggs at the smaller end.

Big-Endians

Exiled to Blefuscu · their books banned

vs

Little-Endians

The emperor's law in Lilliput

Their holy book only says: "all true believers break their eggs at the convenient end." Which end is convenient? Nobody can agree.

Decoding the satire

Swift's first readers in 1726 would have recognised these jokes immediately.

In Lilliput……Swift is mocking
Lilliput vs. Blefuscu→England vs. France, two rival neighbours always at war
Big-Endians vs. Little-Endians→Catholics vs. Protestants: bloody fights over religious details
High Heels vs. Low Heels (Tramecksan & Slamecksan)→Tories vs. Whigs, England's two political parties. The prince wears one high heel and one low, so he wobbles.
Rope-dancing for jobs→Politicians who win power by clever tricks, not by being good at the job
Flimnap, the Treasurer→Probably Robert Walpole, Britain's powerful first minister
Tiny people, huge pride→Human self-importance. From high enough up, our quarrels look ridiculous
II
Part II · A Voyage to Brobdingnag

The tiny man among giants

Only two months after getting home, Gulliver sails again. This time he's the one who's pocket-sized: a toy, a pet and a curiosity, in a land of wise giants.

8 chaptersShip: the AdventureStay: about 2 yearsHis nickname: Grildrig, "mannikin"

The story in eight beats

  1. Ch. 1

    Abandoned on shore

    Exploring for water, Gulliver's shipmates see a giant and row off without him. A farmhand picks him up between finger and thumb.

  2. Ch. 1

    The farmer's house

    He fights two rats as big as mastiffs with his sword, and is nearly swallowed by a baby. Everything he loved about being big is reversed.

  3. Ch. 2

    Glumdalclitch

    The farmer's 9-year-old daughter becomes his "little nurse." She names him Grildrig and teaches him the language.

  4. Ch. 2–3

    Shown for money, then sold

    The farmer exhibits him in towns until he's half-starved, then sells him to the queen for 1,000 gold pieces. Glumdalclitch stays as his nurse.

  5. Ch. 3–5

    Dangers at court

    The jealous queen's dwarf drops him in a bowl of cream and jams him in a marrow-bone. Wasps, a frog, a linnet and a monkey all attack him. The monkey carries him onto a roof!

  6. Ch. 6

    Explaining England

    Over five or six audiences, Gulliver proudly describes England's parliament, courts and wars. The king asks sharp questions and pronounces his famous verdict.

  7. Ch. 7

    The gunpowder offer

    Hoping to impress, Gulliver offers to make cannons for the king. The king is horrified and forbids him ever to mention it.

  8. Ch. 8

    Carried off by an eagle

    An eagle grabs his travelling box and drops it in the sea. An English ship rescues him, and for weeks at home everyone looks like a pygmy to him.

I cannot but conclude the bulk of your natives to be the most pernicious race of little odious vermin that nature ever suffered to crawl upon the surface of the earth. The King of Brobdingnag, after hearing Gulliver praise England · Part II, Ch. 6
Explainer

Why the giant king is the hero of Part II

What Gulliver brags about

Parliament, judges, the army, a hundred years of English history, and the power of gunpowder. He thinks he's making England look magnificent.

What the king hears

Bribery, lawyers who twist the law, endless wars, debt, and a history of "conspiracies, rebellions, murders, massacres." He sees the truth Gulliver can't.

What the king values

Common sense and kindness. Whoever could make "two ears of corn, or two blades of grass" grow where one grew before, he says, does more good than all politicians.

Notice the flip The giants aren't perfect. Up close their skin looks blotchy and gross, and a criminal's execution is horrifying. But their king is the wisest ruler Gulliver has met. Being big here means being morally big, and Gulliver's England looks small and nasty beside it.
III
Part III · A Voyage to Laputa, Balnibarbi, Glubbdubdrib, Luggnagg and Japan

Island-hopping among the "experts"

The most scattered voyage: five stops instead of one. The common thread? People who are brilliant in theory and useless, or worse, in real life.

11 chaptersShip: the HopewellTaken by pirates, set adrift in a canoeFive stops

The route, stop by stop

Stop 1 · Laputa

The flying island of absent-minded thinkers

After pirates (and a nasty Dutchman) set him adrift, Gulliver is hauled up by chain onto Laputa, an island that floats in the sky above the kingdom of Balnibarbi.

Laputans care only about mathematics and music. One head tilts left, one eye turns inward and the other up at the stars. They are so lost in thought that servants called flappers have to bop them with a dried bladder to make them listen. Their tailor measures Gulliver with a quadrant and the suit comes out all wrong. Their houses are badly built because they despise practical geometry.

The island is also a weapon. If a city below rebels, the king parks the island overhead to block sun and rain, or threatens to drop it on them.

Real targetScientists of the Royal Society who loved theory more than useful results, and England's harsh rule over Ireland. The city of Lindalino, which fights back with magnets and wins, may stand for Dublin.
Stop 2 · Balnibarbi and the Academy of Lagado

A whole country ruined by bright ideas

Down on the ground, the capital Lagado is in ruins and the fields are bare. Why? "Projectors" came back from Laputa full of schemes, built an Academy, and made everyone follow their experiments. None of them work. Only old Lord Munodi, who farms the old-fashioned way, has a green, thriving estate, and everyone mocks him for it.

Tap each card to see what the Academy's professors are working on.

Stop 3 · Glubbdubdrib

Dinner with the ghosts of history

The governor of this "island of sorcerers" can summon the dead for 24 hours at a time. Gulliver meets Alexander the Great, Hannibal, Julius Caesar and Brutus, Homer and Aristotle.

The lesson he takes away: history books lie. Famous "great men" were often villains, and many heroes were forgotten. Modern nobles turn out to be descended from servants and crooks.

Stop 4 · Luggnagg

The curse of the Struldbrugs

In Luggnagg, a few babies are born with a spot over the left eyebrow: they are Struldbrugs, and they will never die. Gulliver is thrilled and imagines becoming rich, wise and endlessly learned.

Then he hears the truth: they never die, but they never stop getting older. Drag the slider.

The point Gulliver's dream of living forever was just another "brilliant idea" that falls apart in real life, like the cucumber sunbeams. After meeting the Struldbrugs, he says no tyrant could invent a death he wouldn't gladly run to instead.

On the last leg, he sails to Japan pretending to be Dutch, and asks to be excused from the ceremony of trampling on a crucifix (the Emperor finds that suspicious). He gets home through Amsterdam in April 1710.

IV
Part IV · A Voyage to the Country of the Houyhnhnms

Where horses rule and humans are beasts

The darkest and most important voyage. Gulliver finds a society that runs on pure reason, and it belongs to horses. The human-shaped creatures here, the Yahoos, are filthy animals. Gulliver can't stop seeing himself in them.

12 chaptersShip: the Adventurer (he's captain)Stay: about 5 years, his longestSay it: "WHIN-ims"
The masters

Houyhnhnms

Horses who are calm, clean, honest and ruled entirely by reason.

  • No word for lying, so they call it "saying the thing which is not"
  • No war, no money, no lawyers, no government but a general assembly
  • They even "exhort" instead of command
  • They face death calmly and don't grieve much, not even for family
  • Friendly and polite, but cool. Love is a matter of reason
The beasts

Yahoos

Hairy, dirty, human-shaped creatures used as work animals. Gulliver looks just like one.

  • Greedy: they fight over shiny stones they don't need
  • They eat anything and get drunk on a juicy root
  • They follow a leader who is the "most deformed" and keep a favourite who licks his feet
  • Kept tied up in kennels and used to pull carts
  • Gulliver's clothes are the only thing that hides him

The story in eight beats

  1. Ch. 1

    Mutiny and marooning

    Gulliver is captain now, but his crew of ex-buccaneers takes the ship and dumps him on an unknown shore. Yahoos attack him; a dapple-grey horse rescues him.

  2. Ch. 2–3

    A clever Yahoo?

    Taken home by his "master," he lives in a stable on oat-cakes and milk, and learns their neighing language. He hides his body under his clothes.

  3. Ch. 3–4

    "The thing which is not"

    His master can't believe a Yahoo can reason, and can't understand why anyone would lie, or why horses in England are ridden by Yahoos.

  4. Ch. 5–7

    Explaining Europe… again

    Gulliver describes war, lawyers, money, doctors, and prime ministers. His master concludes humans are Yahoos who've used a little reason to make their vices worse.

  5. Ch. 8

    Seeing himself in the Yahoos

    Bathing in a river, he's grabbed by a young female Yahoo. The proof is undeniable: the Yahoos see him as one of their own.

  6. Ch. 9–10

    The assembly's "exhortation"

    The general assembly decides he must be sent away, in case he leads the Yahoos in a raid. Gulliver faints from grief.

  7. Ch. 10–11

    Canoe of Yahoo skins

    With the sorrel nag's help, he builds a canoe, says goodbye, and rows off. Natives in New Holland shoot him in the knee with an arrow.

  8. Ch. 11–12

    Home, but not really

    Kind Portuguese captain Pedro de Mendez brings him home. Gulliver faints when his wife kisses him, buys two horses, and talks to them four hours a day.

As soon as I entered the house, my wife took me in her arms, and kissed me; at which, having not been used to the touch of that odious animal for so many years, I fell into a swoon for almost an hour. Gulliver, returning home in 1715 · Part IV, Ch. 11

Is Gulliver right at the end?

This is the big debate about the book. Gulliver now thinks humans are just Yahoos and worships the horses. But look at the clues Swift leaves:

  • Pedro de Mendez is generous and kind, a human who is nothing like a Yahoo. Gulliver can barely stand to talk to him.
  • The Houyhnhnms are wise, but cold. They plan to wipe out the Yahoos, and they banish Gulliver even though he's done nothing wrong.
  • Gulliver ends up talking to horses in a stable, which is comic in its own way.

Many readers think Swift is warning us twice: humans are proud and vicious, but trying to be a cold, perfect Houyhnhnm is impossible for us, and hating everyone is its own kind of pride.

Putting it all together

How the four voyages connect

The four parts can read like separate adventures, but Swift built them as a set. Each one shows humanity from a new angle, and each one leaves Gulliver less proud of being human.

Pattern 1

The disaster ladder

Look at what strands Gulliver each time. It starts with nature and gets more and more human, ending with his own men turning against him.

By the time he meets the Yahoos, the book has been quietly building its case: the biggest danger to humans is other humans.

Pattern 2

Gulliver's pride, slowly falling

At the start, Gulliver is sure England is the best country on earth. In Lilliput he's still confident, even superior.

In Brobdingnag, the giant king's verdict is the first crack. In Part III, the experts, the ghosts of history and the miserable immortals take more of his faith in human cleverness. In the Houyhnhnm land, the last of his pride is gone: he sees a Yahoo in the mirror.

That's the backbone of the whole book, and it's why the voyages are in this order.

Pattern 3

Two pairs of mirrors

An easy way to remember the structure: the four parts come in two matched pairs.

Lilliput

Gulliver is big. The people are small in body and in mind.

⇄

Brobdingnag

Gulliver is small. The king is big in body and in mind.

Laputa

People who think too much and are useless at living.

⇄

Houyhnhnms

Creatures of perfect reason, but cold, and impossible for a human to become.

Pair one is about size and perspective. Pair two is about reason: bad reason (Laputa) and perfect reason (Houyhnhnms). Humans fail at both ends.
