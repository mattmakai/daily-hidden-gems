How to approach it

Four principles that keep families on the same side

It's their money, and their call

Your role is to support their decisions, not replace them. People who feel respected share more, sooner.

Visibility before control

Start with alerts and view-only access. Take over bill-paying or accounts only when it's truly needed.

Do it while they're well

Legal documents require mental capacity to sign. After a stroke or dementia diagnosis, the only option may be court guardianship.

Write it down, share it

Keep a simple log and keep siblings informed. Transparency prevents the family conflicts that money and caregiving can cause.

Who this is for

This guide is written for families in the United States. Federal rules (Social Security, Medicare, IRS, credit bureaus) apply everywhere; legal documents, probate, and Medicaid rules vary by state. Where it matters, we say so and point you to the official source.
